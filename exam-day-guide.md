# GH-900 Exam-Day Guide

This page covers the logistics of the GitHub Foundations (GH-900) exam: how to book it, the two delivery options, what to have ready, how the exam is built and scored, and what happens after you finish. GitHub certifications are delivered through Microsoft Learn and Pearson VUE, so every fact below links to the official Microsoft Learn page it came from. Policies change, so confirm anything time-sensitive on the linked page before your appointment.

> Also see the [GH-900 practice questions](https://www.savemycert.com/practice/github-foundations/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=gh-900-study-guide) and [timed mock exams](https://www.savemycert.com/mocks/github-foundations/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=gh-900-study-guide).

## Booking the exam

You schedule GH-900 from the Schedule exam section of the certification page on Microsoft Learn, which sends you to Pearson VUE. You sign in to (or create) your Learn profile, add your legal name if it is missing, and fill in your address, phone number and contact email before you are redirected to Pearson VUE to finish.

- **Use a personal Microsoft account.** Microsoft strongly recommends registering with a personal Microsoft account (MSA). If you register with a work or school account, your exam records are lost if you leave that organization, and they cannot be recovered.
- **Your legal name must match your ID.** If the name on your Learn profile does not match your legal ID, you will not be able to take the exam.
- **Price** is based on the country or region where the exam is proctored. Microsoft does not publish a single figure on the certification page, so check the price during scheduling.
- **Languages:** English, Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), Korean and Japanese. If the exam is not available in your preferred language, the study guide says you can request an additional 30 minutes.
- **Scheduling limits:** you can have at most two Microsoft Certification exams scheduled through Pearson VUE at a time, and you can schedule no more than 90 days in advance.
- **Accommodations** (assistive devices, extra time, other changes) must be requested **before** you schedule.

Source: [GitHub Foundations certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/github-foundations/), [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [Study guide for Exam GH-900](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/gh-900)

## Rescheduling and canceling

Reschedule or cancel at least 24 hours before your appointment, or your exam fee is forfeited. If you used a company-purchased voucher, the voucher is forfeited. You reschedule or cancel from the Certifications section of your Learn profile, which sends you to Pearson VUE to complete the change.

Source: [Reschedule and cancellation policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/reschedule-and-cancellation-policy)

## Online proctored vs test center

In most cases you can take the exam online or at a test center. If you don't see an online option, it isn't available from your exam provider.

- **Test center:** a quiet, pre-configured environment. You don't have to test your own computer or install software.
- **Online proctored:** take it almost anywhere, but you are responsible for your computer and room meeting the security rules. A proctor monitors you by webcam and microphone, and the session is recorded.
  - Run the system test on the same computer, network and location you will use, before you register and again on exam day.
  - You can check in from 30 minutes before to 15 minutes after your appointment time. Check-in takes about 15 minutes.
  - The room must be walled with a closed door, and nobody else may enter. Clear the desk of books, notes and writing tools, unplug extra monitors, and keep your phone out of arm's reach but close enough to hear.
  - You may not get help from anyone, or from any AI tool, during the exam.
  - Pearson VUE may use facial comparison to check your face against your ID. If you don't want that, book a test center.
  - Testing on a personal computer is recommended, because work computers often have software that stops the proctoring app from launching.

Source: [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)

## What to have ready

**ID.** You need a current government-issued ID, and the name on it must **exactly** match the name on your Microsoft Certification profile. Legal name-change forms and ID renewal forms are not accepted for online exams. For the full list of acceptable IDs, check Pearson VUE's ID requirements, which are linked from the Microsoft page below.

**Online setup.** Have your mobile phone ready to photograph your ID, your headshot and your exam area during check-in. Make sure your profile has a mobile number with the country code in case the proctor needs to call you. Log in with an account that has local administrator rights and turn off pop-up blockers.

Source: [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)

## How the exam is structured

You have 100 minutes. The exam is proctored and may include interactive components. Microsoft does not publish a question count for GH-900. Its general guidance is that most Microsoft Certification exams contain between 40 and 60 questions, but the number varies by exam. Microsoft does not say which question types appear on an exam. Use the free GitHub exam sandbox to try the interface and question types, and the free practice assessment on the certification page to see the style and difficulty of the questions.

- **No Microsoft Learn access.** Some role-based Microsoft exams let you open Microsoft Learn during the exam. That is not available on GitHub exams.
- **Breaks.** You can take unscheduled breaks, but the exam clock keeps running, and you cannot return to any question you saw before the break.
- **Preview features.** Most questions cover generally available features, but the exam may include preview features that are commonly used.

The skills outline (as of January 2026) weights the seven domains as ranges: Understand Git and GitHub basics (25–30%), Work with GitHub repositories (10–15%), Collaborate using GitHub (10–15%), Apply modern development practices (10–15%), Manage projects with GitHub (5–10%), Understand privacy, security, and administration (10–15%), and Explore the GitHub community (5–10%).

Source: [GitHub Foundations certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/github-foundations/), [Exam duration and exam experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience), [Study guide for Exam GH-900](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/gh-900)

## How scoring works

Scores are reported on a scale of 1 to 1,000, and you need 700 or greater to pass. It is a scaled score, so 700 does not necessarily mean 70% of the points. The passing standard accounts for question difficulty: easier question sets need more points to pass, harder sets need fewer.

- **Partial credit.** On most multi-part questions you get one point for each correct part, so you can earn all, some or none of the points.
- **No penalty for guessing.** A wrong answer earns nothing for that part, but no points are deducted, so answer every question.
- **Unscored questions.** Some questions may be unscored while Microsoft gathers data on them. You won't know which ones, so treat every question as scored.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports)

## Results and your certification

For most exams, results arrive within minutes of finishing, together with a score report. Your score is available in your Learn profile within 24 hours. The report shows your overall numeric score, pass or fail status, and a bar chart of your performance in each skill area. The bars show relative strengths and weaknesses. You can't use them to count correct answers, and Microsoft does not tell you which questions you got wrong.

The GitHub Foundations certification does not require renewal. It follows Microsoft's policy for fundamentals-level exams, which have no recertification option.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports), [GitHub Foundations certification page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/github-foundations/)

## If you don't pass

- After a first failed attempt, you can retake the exam after 24 hours.
- Each later attempt needs a 14-day wait.
- You can take the same exam at most five times in 12 months from your first attempt.
- You pay for each retake (if applicable).
- You can't retake an exam you have already passed unless your certification has expired.

The waiting period is waived only when an internet connectivity problem or equipment failure during the exam was logged in a Pearson VUE case.

Source: [Exam retake policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy)

## On exam day: practical tips

- **Pace yourself.** With 100 minutes, you have plenty of time if you keep moving. Mark hard questions for review rather than stalling on them.
- **Answer everything.** There is no penalty for guessing, so never leave a question blank.
- **Review before any break.** You can't go back to earlier questions after a break, so answer and review what you have seen before taking one.
- **Read for the constraint.** GH-900 options are often near-twins that all exist on GitHub. The deciding detail is usually something like least access, who owns the repository, where the work must run, or "without rewriting history".
